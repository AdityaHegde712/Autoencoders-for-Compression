### The Findings: "The Frame-by-Frame Backward"
Inside the training loop, I added this line:
```python
total_step_loss.backward() # <--- Calling backward for every frame
...
f_hat_prev = f_hat_curr.detach() # <--- Detaching the graph
```

**What this is doing:** 
Because we call `.backward()` after **every single frame** and then **`.detach()`** the reconstruction, PyTorch calculates the gradients for Frame 1 and then **immediately clears the memory** before it even looks at Frame 2.

*   **Memory Usage**: It will *always* stay at 3GB, whether the sequence is 4 frames or 1,000 frames, because it's only ever "holding" one frame of activations at a time.
*   **The Problem**: The model is learning to compress Frame 2 effectively, but it's **not** learning to optimize Frame 1 *in a way that makes Frame 2 easier to compress.* There's no "temporal gradient" flowing back through the sequence.

### Your Goal: "Backpropagation Through Time" (BPTT)
If you want the model to learn **true temporal strategy** (where it might sacrifice a few bits on the I-frame to save a huge amount of bits on the next 9 P-frames), we need to change two things:

1.  **Stop Detaching**: Remove `.detach()` so the gradient for Frame 10 can flow all the way back to Frame 1.
2.  **Accumulate Loss**: Only call `.backward()` **once** at the very end of the 10-frame sequence.

### The Impact of this Change:
*   **Memory**: This **will** maximize your resource usage. A sequence of 10 will now take **roughly 10x the memory** of a sequence of 1 (instead of 1x). 
*   **Performance**: This is "Level 2" neural video compression. The model actually learns to be "smart" about the future of the sequence.
